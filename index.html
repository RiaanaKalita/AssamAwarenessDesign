The river that never really leaves

The Brahmaputra is one of Asia's largest rivers, flowing roughly 900 km through Assam before it empties into the Bay of Bengal. Nearly 40% of the state's land is flood-prone — close to four times the national average — and the river floods in some form almost every monsoon.

2022 alone: ~5.5 million people affected across 32 of Assam's 35 districts.

Standing on a fault line

Assam sits in Seismic Zone V — India's highest earthquake-risk classification — right where the Indian and Eurasian tectonic plates collide. The 1950 Assam–Tibet earthquake, magnitude ~8.6, remains one of the most powerful earthquakes ever recorded on land.

Zone V covers the entire Northeast — the same region carrying the Brahmaputra's floodwaters.

When both hit at once

An earthquake can crack the embankments built to hold back flood water. Roads and bridges damaged by tremors can block flood evacuation routes. Emergency crews trained and equipped for one hazard can be stretched thin covering both — and it's usually the same low-lying, riverside villages exposed to each.

Compound risk isn't hypothetical here — it's a planning problem the state actually has to solve.
