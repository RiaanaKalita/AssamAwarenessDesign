Why does Assam flood?

Assam sits in the Brahmaputra valley, one of the wettest parts of India. Every monsoon, heavy rain in Assam and in the hills upstream pours huge amounts of water into the Brahmaputra and its many tributaries.

Monsoon rain

From June to September, rain fills the rivers faster than they can drain. Rain in Arunachal Pradesh and Bhutan also flows down into Assam.

Erosion and silt

Rivers eat away their banks and drop silt that raises the riverbed, so water spills over more easily. Majuli, a river island, has been shrinking for decades.

Climate change

A warming climate can bring heavier bursts of rain, so floods can arrive faster and rise higher.

Living by the river

Fertile land beside the river attracts farms and villages, so many families are in the path of the water.

How floods change lives

Rescue teams use boats to reach families cut off by water.

Villagers float belongings on rafts made of banana trunks.

Flooded roads trap vehicles and cut off whole villages.

Homes

Houses are damaged or washed away. Families move to relief camps in schools and on raised embankments.

Health

Dirty water spreads diarrhoea, cholera and typhoid. Snakes and insects enter homes, and small cuts get infected.

Food and income

Paddy fields, vegetable plots and livestock are lost, so families can lose their income for months.

School

Schools close or become relief camps, so children miss weeks of learning.

Wildlife

At Kaziranga National Park, animals escape to higher ground and busy highways, where they are in danger.

Myths and facts

If the water is shallow, it is safe to walk through.

Fact: 6 inches of moving water can knock you down, and it hides open drains, sharp objects and live wires.

A hospital is too far away, so I cannot get help.

Fact: Call 112 or 108 first. The Safety page shows your nearest hospital.

Floodwater that looks clear is safe to drink.

Fact: It carries germs you cannot see. Drink only boiled or purified water.

There is nothing I can do in a disaster.

Fact: A packed kit, the right numbers and moving to high ground early save lives.

Flood safety steps

Before a floodGet ready

  1. Know your high ground. Find the nearest raised land, school or relief camp and the safest way to walk there.
  2. Pack a waterproof bag. Use the checklist below and keep it near the door.
  3. Save the numbers. Put 112 and 1070 in your phone and write them on paper too.
  4. Protect your papers. Keep IDs and medical records in a sealed plastic pouch, and take photos of them.
  5. Make a family plan. Pick a meeting point in case phones stop working.

During a floodStay safe

  1. Move early. Go to high ground as soon as the water rises. Do not wait for it to enter your home.
  2. Stay out of moving water. 6 inches can knock you down and 1 foot can sweep a car away.
  3. Switch off power. Turn off the main switch and gas only if you can reach them without standing in water.
  4. Stay informed. Listen to the radio, local announcements and the Sachet app.
  5. Call for help. Dial 112 and share your location. Keep calls short to save battery.

After a floodRecover safely

  1. Wait for the all-clear. Go home only when authorities say it is safe.
  2. Avoid standing water. It can hide snakes, sharp objects and live wires.
  3. Drink safe water. Boil or purify water until officials say the tap water is safe.
  4. Check for injuries. See a doctor for cuts, fever or stomach illness. Use the hospital locator above.
  5. Clean and dry. Dry your home to stop mould, and never run a generator indoors.
